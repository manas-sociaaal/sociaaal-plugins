# Remindful — Research Dossier

## App at a glance
Milestone reminder app. Never forget anniversaries, birthdays, occasions. Productivity category. Light AI-fit — screen recordings + AI clock/calendar B-roll.

## Audience profile
- **Primary:** Busy adults (28–50), partners in long relationships, parents with kid milestones, professionals managing client / team birthdays.
- **Secondary:** Entrepreneurs maintaining customer-anniversary outreach, teachers tracking student birthdays.
- **Where they live:** TikTok #relationshiphack / #productivityhack, Reddit r/relationships, parenting Facebook groups.
- **Motivation:** Avoid the disaster of forgetting an anniversary. Save partner-relationship points. Keep professional relationships warm.

## Core promise
**Never forget the important date again.**

## Value props (ranked)
1. Multi-tier reminder system (1 month before, 1 week, 1 day, day-of)
2. Auto-fill suggestions from contacts / calendar
3. Gift-prompt suggestions
4. Cross-relationship coverage (partner, family, friends, professional)

## Cultural moments to ride
- "Husband / boyfriend forgot anniversary" content (perpetually viral, relatable)
- Mother's Day / Father's Day / Valentine's Day / specific anniversary cultural beats
- "Productivity hack" content seasons (January planning, September reset)
- Wedding-anniversary statistics moments (couples saying "I only made it because…")

## Format playbook
**Works:** POV "husband saved by app", screen recording of setup, talking-head hack format, "Saved by Remindful" testimonial.
**Doesn't:** abstract storytelling — the value prop is concrete + practical.
**AI-asset fit:** Light. Mostly screen recordings. AI for calendar / clock motion graphics, B-roll of relationships (couples at dinner, family birthday, etc).

## Competitor context
Differentiate from Apple Reminders (built-in but generic), Google Calendar (no relationship-specific tooling), Birthdate (similar feature, less polished). Remindful's lane is **dedicated relationship-milestone reminder app with rich UI**.

## Don't-do list
- Don't shame the audience ("you're a bad partner if you forget"). Frame as "don't worry, we got you."
- Avoid stereotyping (not all forgetful partners are husbands).
- Don't fabricate testimonial-style content with fake "saved my marriage" claims.
