# HeroMe — Research Dossier

## App at a glance
AI superhero photo transformation. Selfie → comic / movie superhero rendering. Photo-AI category. Excellent AI-fit — full hero stills via NanoBanana, dramatic motion via Kling.

## Audience profile
- **Primary:** Marvel / DC fans, kids and teens (10–22), gaming-adjacent users, fandom communities.
- **Secondary:** Birthday-gift parents, cosplay enthusiasts, content creators making transformation reaction videos.
- **Where they live:** TikTok #aiart / #superhero / #marvelai, Reddit r/marvelmemes / r/dccomics, Instagram fan-art accounts.
- **Motivation:** See yourself as a superhero. Parents making fun custom photos for kids. Cosplay reference. Transformation entertainment.

## Core promise
**See yourself as a superhero in seconds.**

## Value props (ranked)
1. Comic-quality output — feels like a real comic panel
2. Variety of hero styles (Marvel-vibe, DC-vibe, original characters)
3. Fast generation (under a minute)
4. Shareable as profile picture / printable

## Past creative observations
- P0_X folder in `NEW PHASE GATE/` (may exist or may not — check on next run).
- Likely formats: selfie → hero morph, comic panel reveal, "hero of the week" weekly drops.

## Cultural moments to ride
- Superhero movie release cycles (every 2–3 months when major Marvel/DC films drop)
- Comic Con seasons (NYCC October, San Diego July)
- Halloween (cosplay-curious audience peaks)
- Gaming releases that feature superhero themes

## Format playbook
**Works:** selfie-to-hero reveal, morph transformation, comic panel motion, before/after dramatic transformation.
**Doesn't:** dialogue-heavy, voice-led content.
**AI-asset fit:** Excellent. Full hero stills via NanoBanana with comic styling, dramatic Kling motion (cape billow, lightning, action poses).

## Competitor context
Differentiate from Lensa AI (broader filters, photo-stylization), Reface (face-swap into existing footage), Picsart (DIY tools). HeroMe's lane is **dedicated superhero stylization with comic-quality output**.

## Don't-do list
- Don't depict copyrighted characters too literally (Iron Man's exact suit, Captain America's exact shield). Use vibes, not direct IP.
- Avoid violent superhero scenarios.
- Avoid pairing user with a clearly aged-down character (could read as inappropriate).

## Open research questions
- Does the audience prefer Marvel-vibe vs DC-vibe vs original-character styling?
- Is there crossover with anime-styling content? Could that drive a sub-feature?
- Halloween push — what's the right ramp window (start mid-September)?
