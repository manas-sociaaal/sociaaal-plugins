# Fontkey — Research Dossier

## App at a glance
Fonts keyboard with emoji. Aesthetic fonts + emoji combos for social/messaging. Keyboard-aesthetic category. Sister app to Fontmaker — broader emoji play, similar core mechanic.

## Audience profile
- **Primary:** Gen Z social-media users (14–22), similar to Fontmaker's audience but with stronger emoji-combo culture.
- **Secondary:** People decorating bios/captions with both fonts AND emojis, group-chat aesthetic communicators.
- **Where they live:** TikTok #aestheticfont / #emojicombos / #keyboardhack, Instagram bio-decoration culture, Pinterest aesthetic boards.
- **Motivation:** Express through both fonts and emojis. Stand out in group chats. Decorate bios with combos.

## Core promise
**Express yourself with fonts AND emojis in any app.**

## Value props (ranked)
1. Hundreds of fonts (overlapping with Fontmaker library)
2. Curated emoji combos / emoji aesthetic packs
3. One-tap insert in any app
4. Trending emoji combinations updated regularly

## Cultural moments to ride
- Specific emoji-combo trend cycles ("✨ this emoji combo means…")
- Bio aesthetic seasons (Y2K revival, soft girl, dark academia)
- Back-to-school content
- Specific emoji moments going viral (e.g. an emoji that gains new meaning culturally)

## Format playbook
**Works:** screen recording text transformation, POV bio reveal, before/after emoji-decoration glow-up.
**Doesn't:** live action, complex storytelling, voice-led content.
**AI-asset fit:** Light — screen-first like Fontmaker. AI for B-roll vibe (aesthetic phone-on-desk, study setups, coffee shop vibes).

## Competitor context
Differentiate from Fontmaker (Sociaaal sister app — Fontmaker is fonts-led, Fontkey is fonts+emojis). Differentiate from Cool Fonts and Fonts Aa (free competitors). Fontkey's lane is **fonts + curated emoji combos in one keyboard**.

## Cannibalization risk vs Fontmaker
Real consideration — they target overlapping audiences. Possible angles:
- Fontmaker = "aesthetic fonts focus"
- Fontkey = "fonts + emoji combos focus"
- OR: position one as iOS-first, other as cross-platform
- Discuss with product before running parallel campaigns to the same audience

## Don't-do list
- Don't show emoji combinations that have problematic cultural meanings.
- Don't run identical creative to Fontmaker — they need differentiated positioning.
- Avoid screen-recording with notification banners.

## Open research questions

- Is the emoji-combo angle the real wedge, or do users just want fonts?
- Cross-cohort conversion (Fontmaker user → Fontkey user) — happens naturally?
